I created this guide because I struggled to find clear advice on choosing AI models for the work I actually do. There are plenty of rankings, but understanding which benchmarks matter for a financial model, industry research or a long report takes more digging than it should.

In my experience, benchmark pages often assume technical knowledge and can be confusing for non-technical users. This guide draws on my own use of AI in Treasury and finance. It explains the relevant tests in ordinary language and links them to familiar tasks, so other knowledge workers have a useful place to start.

The overall Intelligence Index combines professional tasks, coding, scientific reasoning and general capabilities. Its weighting may not reflect your workload, which is why this guide focuses on the benchmarks relevant to each task. Read the index methodology ↗

Choose models for the work you do

I’ve reviewed the benchmarks and tried the models in my own work to put this guide together. I’ll update it periodically as models and results change. You may want to bookmark this page and come back when you’re choosing a model for a new task.

Overall recommendation · Based on the 1 October review

Claude Opus 5.5 as the default.
Sonnet 5.5 when turnaround matters.

My 1 October review recommended Opus for a mix of Treasury and finance knowledge work within a five-model shortlist, with Sonnet when turnaround matters. The review recorded a much longer wait for Opus at maximum effort. This is a dated workload judgement, rather than a new assessment of cost and speed.

Use Fable 5.1 for a quantitative second opinion, Astra at xhigh for long-PDF trials, and Gemini 3.8 Flash for routine capture and first-pass work. These are workload recommendations. Specialist benchmark winners may sit outside this shortlist.

Benchmark definitions

What the benchmarks measure (if you’d like to read more)

Check the definition before comparing scores. A relative rating, a completion rate and a consistency measure answer different questions.

AA-Briefcase

Analysis, spreadsheets and written deliverables

Evaluates professional projects with memos, presentations and spreadsheets. Combines task completion, analytical quality and presentation.

Limit: A combined score does not isolate financial modelling quality or validate institutional judgement.

View benchmark and results ↗
GDPval-AA

Professional tasks across occupations

Tests occupational work products in a defined tool environment. Offers a second perspective on professional execution.

Limit: The tested tools and environment may differ from the application you use.

View benchmark and results ↗
AA-AnalystAgent

Consistent quantitative analysis

Works from spreadsheets and documents. Its headline pass^5 score requires a question to be answered correctly on all five attempts.

Limit: Numerical correctness does not establish that a workbook has sound formulas, assumptions and scenario behaviour.

View benchmark and results ↗
AA-LCR

Reasoning across long documents

Tests extraction, synthesis and reasoning across long inputs, including financial and policy documents.

Limit: Accepting a long input is different from reasoning reliably across it. Check source fidelity yourself.

View benchmark and results ↗
GDP.pdf

Analysis of long professional PDFs

Tests answers grounded in long professional documents. The headline all-pass score requires every criterion for an attempt to pass.

Limit: This is not a simple figure-extraction accuracy rate or a guarantee of flawless PDF handling in your app.

View benchmark and results ↗
AA-Omniscience

Factual knowledge and incorrect answers

Rewards correct answers, penalises incorrect guesses and leaves abstention neutral.

Limit: It does not guarantee current information or verify every citation in a particular response.

View benchmark and results ↗
AutomationBench-AA

Workflows across business applications

Measures objectives completed across applications without guardrail violations.

Limit: It does not validate autonomous operation of a Treasury system.

Verification

Checks for financial models, source data and PDFs

These practical checks are suggested for finance work. They are not claims about the tasks contained in the benchmarks.

Financial models

Change an input. Inspect dependent outputs. Test a period boundary and downside scenario. Check that formulas remain live and totals reconcile.

Industry source data

Require source locations, units, dates and definitions. Distinguish outstanding balances from planned issuance, averages from period-end values, and accounting from regulatory measures.

PDF interpretation

Ask for the exact evidence supporting the conclusion. Check table footnotes, reporting scope, conflicting passages and what the document leaves unanswered.
