For bankers, AI announcements are easy to find. Understanding which institutions have built the people, research and leadership to support them takes more work. The Evident AI Index offers a structured starting point for that comparison.

Its value is in the questions it helps you ask. Where is a bank building capability? What does it disclose about deployment and controls? Which peers are worth studying more closely?

How the assessment works

Evident assesses banks from the outside, using public disclosures, company reporting and third-party sources. It combines manual research with automated data collection. The four pillars cover AI staffing and development, innovation, executive priorities and openness about responsible AI. Evident’s methodology overview.

The 2026 global report describes 75 indicators; the MEA methodology specifies 65. Both use four pillars, with different weights:

The dates, bank samples and weights differ. First place in MEA cannot be treated as equivalent to first place globally. A pillar rank is an ordinal position, not a percentage score or a measure of the gap between banks.

How bankers can use it

Start with peers relevant to your bank’s market and business model. Then look below the overall rank: a strong talent base, a research programme and clear responsible AI disclosures point to different things worth learning.

Follow the evidence back to the bank’s own reports. If a peer describes an AI use case, ask what changed: time taken, errors, customer outcomes or cost. Look for the measurement period and whether the benefit is realised or projected.

This matters because Evident’s global findings say only 12% of use cases report impact against operational KPIs, and barely 1% disclose concrete financial returns. A high maturity rank should still prompt a separate question about demonstrated value. 2026 global findings.

For regional context, Evident identifies talent as a constraint to scaling AI in MEA. It reports that average AI development talent relative to headcount is half that in its global benchmark. That is a finding from the June report, rather than a comparison with the later October ranking. MEA findings.

What the ranking leaves open

An assessment based on public information has a visibility limit. Internal progress may be undisclosed; disclosures may describe ambitions more clearly than results. That is a reason to investigate a low rank, rather than conclude that a bank has little AI capability.

The index is also a selected sample. A bank’s absence does not establish poor performance. Nor does a high Transparency rank certify that every AI system is safe or that a bank meets every regulatory requirement.

My reading: use Evident to identify peers and sharpen questions about capability, deployment and evidence. An internal assessment still needs your bank’s own data on adoption, benefits and controls.
